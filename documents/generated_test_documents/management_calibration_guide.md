# Team Performance Calibration Guide — Management Confidential

**Classification: Management Confidential — For people managers participating in calibration sessions only.**

## What is Calibration?

Performance calibration is a structured process where managers collectively review and align performance ratings across teams to ensure consistency, fairness, and adherence to the rating distribution guidelines. Calibration prevents rating inflation and ensures that high performers are recognized while underperformance is addressed.

## Calibration Process

### Step 1: Independent Rating (Week 1)
- Each manager independently rates their direct reports using the 5-point performance scale.
- Ratings must be supported by specific examples and documented evidence.
- Managers should not discuss their ratings with other managers before calibration.

### Step 2: Calibration Session (Week 2)
- HR facilitates a calibration meeting for each business unit / department.
- Attendees: All people managers in the department + HR Business Partner + Department Head.
- Each manager presents their proposed ratings with justification.
- The group discusses borderline cases and ensures consistency across teams.

### Step 3: Final Ratings and Communication (Week 3)
- Final calibrated ratings are locked in the HR system.
- Managers communicate ratings to their reports in 1-on-1 feedback sessions.
- Employees may submit an appeal within 10 working days of receiving their rating.

## Calibration Discussion Guidelines

### Questions to Ask
- "What specific deliverables differentiate this employee from others at the same rating?"
- "How does this employee compare to peers at the same level across the department?"
- "Is this rating consistent with the employee's trajectory over the last 2 cycles?"

### Common Calibration Pitfalls to Avoid
1. **Recency Bias**: Rating based on the last 4-6 weeks instead of the full review period.
2. **Halo Effect**: Letting one strength or weakness dominate the overall rating.
3. **Leniency Bias**: Giving everyone a 3 or 4 to avoid difficult conversations.
4. **Central Tendency**: Clustering all ratings around 3 regardless of actual performance.
5. **Similarity Bias**: Rating employees higher because they remind the manager of themselves.

## Rating Distribution Targets

The following distribution is a guideline, not a strict quota. Significant deviations must be justified during calibration.

| Rating | Target Distribution |
|--------|-------------------|
| 5 — Exceptional | 5-10% |
| 4 — Exceeds Expectations | 20-25% |
| 3 — Meets Expectations | 45-55% |
| 2 — Needs Improvement | 10-15% |
| 1 — Unsatisfactory | 3-5% |

## Promotion Case Presentation

During calibration, managers may present promotion cases for employees they believe are ready for the next level. A promotion case must include:

1. Evidence of sustained performance at the next level for at least 6 months.
2. Endorsement from the skip-level manager.
3. Assessment against the competency framework for the target level.
4. Business justification (role scope expansion, team growth, or strategic need).

## Confidentiality

All calibration discussions, individual ratings, and promotion decisions are strictly confidential. Managers must not reveal the ratings of other employees or share calibration session discussions with their teams.
